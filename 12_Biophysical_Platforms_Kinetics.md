## 1. SPR (Surface Plasmon Resonance)

### Principle
SPR monitors changes in the refractive index at a gold-coated sensor chip surface caused by biomolecular binding. When polarized light hits the gold film at a specific angle, it excites surface plasmons (collective electron oscillations). Binding of analyte molecules to immobilized ligand on the chip surface changes the local refractive index, shifting the resonance angle. This angular shift is directly proportional to the mass of bound molecules and is recorded in real time as a sensorgram (response units, RU, vs. time).

- **No labeling required** -- the measurement is label-free
- One binding partner (ligand) is immobilized on the sensor chip; the other (analyte) flows over the surface in a microfluidic system
- The sensorgram captures association (analyte binding, increasing signal) and dissociation (analyte unbinding during buffer wash, decreasing signal) phases

### Kinetic Parameters Measured
| Parameter | Symbol | Units | Description |
|-----------|--------|-------|-------------|
| Association rate constant | ka (or kon) | M-1 s-1 | Rate of complex formation |
| Dissociation rate constant | kd (or koff) | s-1 | Rate of complex dissociation |
| Equilibrium dissociation constant | KD | M (nM) | kd/ka -- lower KD = higher affinity |
| Equilibrium association constant | KA | M-1 | ka/kd = 1/KD |

**Both kinetic (ka, kd) and equilibrium (KD) parameters are obtained from a single experiment** by fitting sensorgrams to binding models (typically 1:1 Langmuir).

### Throughput
- **Biacore T200 (single-channel):** ~20-50 full kinetic titrations per day using multi-cycle kinetics (MCK). Single-cycle kinetics (SCK) can increase throughput by eliminating regeneration between concentrations.
- **Biacore 8K (8-channel):** Up to 64 affinity titrations in 3-5 hours; ~128 titrations per day; up to 2,300 single-concentration screening runs per day from 384-well plates. Supports continuous operation for up to 60 hours with four 96- or 384-well plates.
- Sensor chips can typically be regenerated 50-100 times (depends on ligand stability and regeneration conditions).

**Source:** Cytiva Biacore 8K product data sheets; SickKids SBC Facility Biacore 8K page; Sygnature Discovery press release on Biacore 8K.

### Sample Requirements
- **Protein purity:** >95% purity recommended; monodisperse (no aggregates) is critical
- **Quantity per experiment:** Typically 5-50 micrograms of analyte per full kinetic titration; immobilized ligand requires ~1-10 micrograms
- **Concentration range for kinetics:** Analyte concentrations should span ~0.1x to 10x the expected KD (e.g., for a 5 nM KD: use 0.5-50 nM analyte)
- **Buffer requirements:** Running buffer compatible with microfluidics (degassed, filtered); HBS-EP+ (10 mM HEPES, 150 mM NaCl, 3 mM EDTA, 0.05% P20) is standard
- **Volume per injection:** ~60-100 microliters at standard flow rates (30 microliters/min)

**Source:** Cytiva product pages; Creative Proteomics Biacore instrument comparison guide.

### Advantages
1. **Gold standard** for binding kinetics -- accepted by FDA/EMA for regulatory submissions
2. Real-time measurement of both association and dissociation kinetics
3. Label-free detection
4. Excellent sensitivity: can detect binding of molecules as small as ~100-200 Da
5. Wide KD range: pM to mM (with different experimental designs)
6. Highly reproducible; extensive literature for benchmarking
7. Single-cycle kinetics eliminates need for regeneration
8. Multiple software tools for complex binding model fitting

### Limitations
1. **Microfluidics system** -- requires degassed, filtered buffers; prone to clogging with impure samples
2. **Immobilization artifacts:** One partner must be immobilized, which can affect binding if the immobilization site is near the binding interface
3. **Mass transport effects:** If analyte delivery is slower than binding, kinetics are distorted
4. **Avidity effects:** If ligand density is too high or analyte is multivalent, apparent affinity is overestimated
5. **Sensor chips are expensive:** ~$200-$600 per chip depending on type
6. **Instrument cost is very high** (see below)
7. **Cannot measure binding in membrane context** -- both partners are in solution/on surface, not in native membrane environment

## 2. BLI (Bio-Layer Interferometry)

### Principle
BLI is an optical biosensing technique that measures molecular interactions by monitoring changes in the interference pattern of white light reflected from two surfaces at the tip of a fiber-optic biosensor: (1) an internal reference layer and (2) a bio-layer where the ligand is immobilized. When analyte binds to the ligand on the biosensor tip, the optical thickness of the bio-layer increases, causing a measurable wavelength shift in the reflected light interference pattern. This shift (measured in nanometers, nm) is plotted in real time.

**Key difference from SPR:** BLI uses a "dip-and-read" format -- the biosensor tips are dipped into sample-containing wells of a standard microplate. There is **no microfluidic system**. Binding is driven by orbital shaking of the plate rather than laminar flow.

### Kinetic Parameters Measured
Same as SPR: **ka (kon), kd (koff), KD** from real-time binding sensorgrams fitted to kinetic models.

### Throughput
- **Octet R2 (2-channel):** Entry-level; low throughput; suitable for method development
- **Octet R4 (4-channel):** Mid-range
- **Octet R8 (8-channel):** Runs 8 biosensors simultaneously; can process a 96-well plate in ~30-60 minutes per kinetic step; ~50-100 kinetic titrations per day
- **Octet RH16 (16-channel):** Higher throughput
- **Octet RH96 (96-channel):** Ultra-high throughput; 96 samples simultaneously; can screen thousands of interactions per day

**BLI generally offers higher throughput than single-channel SPR systems** because multiple sensors run in parallel without microfluidics setup time.

### Sample Requirements
- **Protein purity:** >90% recommended; aggregation affects data quality
- **Quantity:** Similar to SPR; ~5-50 micrograms per analyte titration; loading ligand on biosensor tips requires ~5-20 micrograms
- **Concentration range:** Same principle as SPR -- analyte concentrations should span ~0.1x to 10x KD
- **Volume:** 200 microliters per well in 96-well plates (can use lower volumes in 384-well plates)
- **Buffer:** More flexible than SPR -- crude samples (cell lysates, serum) can be used with appropriate biosensor tips

### Advantages Over SPR
1. **No microfluidics** -- no clogging, no need for degassed/filtered buffers
2. **Can work with crude/complex matrices** (cell lysates, conditioned media) because the biosensor tip is dipped and removed
3. **Higher inherent throughput** with parallel sensor operation
4. **Lower maintenance costs** -- no fluidics system to maintain
5. **Faster turnaround** for routine assays
6. **Plate-based format** integrates easily with automated liquid handling

### Limitations Compared to SPR
1. **~100-fold lower sensitivity** than SPR -- difficult to detect binding of small molecules (<1 kDa); practical lower limit for analyte MW is ~150 Da for advanced systems, but SPR is generally superior for small molecule work
2. **Less precise kinetic data** for very slow off-rates (kd < 10^-5 s-1) due to baseline drift from non-specific binding and sensor tip variability
3. **Biosensor tips are single-use** (not regenerable like SPR chips) -- each tip costs ~$5-$15
4. **Sensor-to-sensor variability** can be higher than SPR chip-to-chip variability
5. **Shaking artifacts:** If mixing is insufficient, mass transport effects arise
6. **Regulatory acceptance:** SPR (Biacore) is more widely accepted by regulatory agencies as the gold standard

## 3. MST (Microscale Thermophoresis)

### Principle
MST measures the directed movement of molecules along a microscopic temperature gradient (thermophoresis). A focused infrared (IR) laser creates a local temperature increase of 1-5 degrees C inside a thin glass capillary. The movement of fluorescently labeled molecules in this gradient depends on their size, charge, and hydration shell. When a ligand binds, at least one of these properties changes, altering the thermophoretic behavior. The change in fluorescence distribution is monitored and used to determine binding.

The measurement requires one binding partner to be fluorescently labeled (either intrinsic fluorescence, e.g., tryptophan, or extrinsic labeling with a dye).

### What It Measures
| Parameter | Measured? | Notes |
|-----------|-----------|-------|
| KD (equilibrium) | **Yes** | From dose-response curve (fluorescence change vs. ligand concentration) |
| ka / kon | **No** | MST measures equilibrium, not kinetics |
| kd / koff | **No** | Cannot extract individual rate constants |
| Stoichiometry | Partially | Can infer from curve fitting |

**MST provides only equilibrium KD, NOT individual kinetic rate constants (kon, koff).** This is a critical limitation for projects that need to distinguish variants by their kinetic profiles (e.g., fast-on/fast-off vs. slow-on/slow-off).

### Advantages
1. **Truly in-solution measurement** -- no immobilization of either partner; eliminates surface artifacts
2. **Very low sample consumption:** ~10-20 microliters per capillary at ~10-100 nM labeled molecule; a full 16-point titration uses ~200-400 microliters total
3. **Wide KD range:** pM to mM
4. **Works in complex matrices:** Serum, cell lysates, detergent solutions
5. **Fast:** A single titration takes ~15-30 minutes
6. **No immobilization artifacts** -- particularly valuable for confirming SPR/BLI results

### Limitations
1. **Cannot measure kinetics** (kon, koff individually) -- only equilibrium KD
2. **Requires fluorescent labeling** of one partner (unless using intrinsic tryptophan fluorescence in the Monolith NT.Pico)
3. **Aggregation-sensitive:** Aggregated protein can produce artifacts
4. **Capillary adsorption:** Hydrophobic molecules may stick to capillary walls (premium coated capillaries partially mitigate this)
5. **Not regulatory gold standard** -- SPR is preferred for regulatory filings
6. **Data interpretation can be ambiguous** if fluorescence changes arise from non-specific effects rather than binding

### Sample Requirements
- **Labeled molecule:** 10-100 nM concentration; ~5-10 microliters per capillary
- **Ligand (titrated partner):** Serial dilution from high concentration (typically 16 points); total ~200-500 microliters
- **Protein purity:** High purity recommended but not strictly required (works in lysates)
- **Labeling:** Typically NHS-ester dye coupling (RED-NHS, NT-647) or His-tag labeling (RED-tris-NTA)

### Published Use for scFv/Antibody Characterization
- MST has been used to measure KD of antibodies and antibody fragments in multiple published studies
- Example: GFP-binding protein (GBP, a nanobody) measured at KD = 2.3 +/- 2.1 nM; a mutant GBP (R37A) showed reduced affinity at KD = 80 +/- 38 nM (Wienken et al., 2010, Nature Communications)
- Affimer proteins (engineered binding proteins similar to nanobodies) characterized by MST showing pM to nM range affinities (NanoTemper application note)
- **No published MST data specifically for FMC63-CD19 interaction was found**

## 4. ITC (Isothermal Titration Calorimetry)

### Principle
ITC directly measures the heat released or absorbed during a binding interaction. One partner (in the cell, ~200-350 microliters) is titrated with the other (from a syringe, ~40 microliters) via small, precisely controlled injections. The instrument measures the differential power required to maintain zero temperature difference between the sample cell and a reference cell. Each injection produces a heat pulse proportional to the amount of binding that occurs. As binding sites become saturated, heat pulses diminish.

### What It Measures
| Parameter | Measured? | Notes |
|-----------|-----------|-------|
| KD | **Yes** | From binding isotherm |
| KA | **Yes** | 1/KD |
| DeltaH (enthalpy) | **Yes** | Directly measured -- unique to ITC |
| DeltaS (entropy) | **Yes** | Calculated from DeltaG and DeltaH |
| DeltaG (free energy) | **Yes** | = -RT ln(KA) |
| Stoichiometry (n) | **Yes** | From inflection point of isotherm |
| ka / kon | **No** | Standard ITC does not measure kinetics |
| kd / koff | **No** | Not measured |

**ITC is the only technique that directly measures thermodynamic parameters (DeltaH, DeltaS) in a single experiment.** This reveals whether binding is enthalpy-driven (hydrogen bonds, van der Waals) or entropy-driven (hydrophobic effect, conformational changes).

### Advantages
1. **No labeling, no immobilization** -- completely in-solution, label-free
2. **Directly measures thermodynamics** -- unique among these techniques
3. **No molecular weight limitation** -- works for any size interaction
4. **Measures stoichiometry** directly
5. **Gold standard for thermodynamic characterization**

### Limitations
1. **Very high protein consumption:** Cell: 200-350 microliters at 10-100 micromolar; Syringe: 40 microliters at 100-1000 micromolar. For a 25 kDa scFv at 50 micromolar, this means ~350-500 micrograms per experiment.
2. **Low throughput:** Each titration takes ~1-2 hours + equilibration; ~5-10 experiments per day maximum
3. **Limited KD range:** Most reliable for KD = 100 nM to 100 micromolar (approximately 10^-5 to 10^-8 M). For very tight binders (KD < 1 nM), the optimal protein concentration is so low that heat changes become undetectable. For very weak binders (KD > 100 micromolar), high concentrations may cause aggregation.
4. **Cannot measure kinetics** (kon, koff)
5. **Entropy-driven interactions produce weak signals:** If DeltaH is small (near zero), the heat pulses may be too small to measure accurately, even for high-affinity interactions
6. **Protein must be highly pure and in matched buffers** -- buffer mismatch creates large heats of dilution that obscure binding heats
7. **Data analysis requires expertise** -- complex binding models (two-site, cooperative) are harder to fit reliably

### When ITC is Useful vs. When SPR/BLI is Better

**Use ITC when:**
- You need thermodynamic data (DeltaH, DeltaS) to understand the energetic basis of binding (e.g., to determine whether a mutation affects enthalpy or entropy)
- You want to confirm stoichiometry
- KD is in the 100 nM to 100 micromolar range
- You have abundant protein available (>500 micrograms per experiment)

**Use SPR/BLI when:**
- You need kinetic rate constants (kon, koff)
- You are screening many variants (throughput matters)
- Protein is limited
- KD is very tight (<10 nM) or you need to distinguish variants by kinetic profile
- Regulatory submission is planned

**For this project (FMC63 affinity variants with site-saturation mutagenesis):** SPR or BLI is far more appropriate than ITC because:
- You need kon and koff to understand kinetic profiles
- You will have many variants to screen (NNK library)
- FMC63 wild-type KD is ~5 nM, which is at the edge of ITC's practical range
- Protein amounts from library screening may be limited

## 5. Flow Cytometry-Based Binding Assays

### Approaches

**Approach A: Fluorescent protein binding to CAR-expressing cells**
- Fluorescently labeled soluble CD19 (e.g., CD19-PE, CD19-APC, or CD19-His + anti-His-fluorochrome) is incubated at varying concentrations with CAR-expressing cells (Jurkat-CAR or primary T-CAR cells)
- After washing, flow cytometry measures the MFI (mean fluorescence intensity) of bound CD19 at each concentration
- Plotting MFI vs. [CD19] gives a saturation binding curve from which an apparent KD is calculated

**Approach B: Soluble scFv binding to CD19+ cells**
- Purified fluorescently labeled scFv (or detected with anti-scFv secondary antibody) at varying concentrations is incubated with CD19+ target cells (e.g., NALM6, Raji)
- Flow cytometry measures bound scFv at each concentration

**Approach C: Doublet formation assay (cellular avidity)**
- CAR-expressing cells (e.g., stable CAR Jurkat) are co-incubated with fluorescently marked target cells at varying E:T ratios for short times
- Flow cytometry measures CAR-target cell doublets (conjugate formation)
- Reported in Galvez-Cancino et al. (2024), Bio-protocol (PMC11211074)

### What It Measures
| Parameter | Measured? | Notes |
|-----------|-----------|-------|
| Apparent KD (on cell surface) | **Yes** | From dose-response saturation curve |
| True KD | **No** | Cell surface KD is affected by avidity, receptor density, membrane context |
| kon / koff | **Not directly** | Though doublet formation kinetics give semi-quantitative association/dissociation info |
| Avidity | **Yes** | Multivalent binding is captured |

### Advantages
1. **Measures binding in cellular context** -- includes effects of receptor density, clustering, membrane organization
2. **Accessible instrumentation** -- most labs have flow cytometers (BD FACSCanto, Fortessa, etc.)
3. **Can assess CAR expression and binding simultaneously** using multi-color panels
4. **Can be performed on live cells** without fixation
5. **High throughput** with plate-based flow cytometry
6. **The doublet assay is functional** -- it measures productive cell-cell interaction, not just molecular binding

### Limitations
1. **Measures apparent KD, not true molecular KD** -- apparent KD on cell surface can differ from solution KD due to avidity effects (multiple CAR molecules engaging multiple CD19 molecules), receptor density differences, and membrane fluidity
2. **Difficult to extract precise kon/koff** from flow cytometry data
3. **Washing steps can disrupt weak interactions** -- low-affinity variants may be underdetected
4. **Fluorescent labeling** required for at least one partner
5. **Standardization is challenging** -- results depend heavily on cell type, CAR expression level, staining protocol, temperature, and incubation time
6. **Not accepted as primary KD measurement** for regulatory or rigorous biophysical characterization

### Published Protocols
- **Galvez-Cancino et al. (2024):** "A Flow Cytometry-Based Method for Assessing CAR Cell Binding Kinetics Using Stable CAR Jurkat Cells." Bio-protocol, vol. 14, issue 12, e5021. PMC11211074. Uses CAR Jurkat cells co-cultured with target cells at varying E:T ratios for short times; measures doublet formation by flow cytometry.
- **Seigner et al. (2023):** Used flow cytometry with fluorescent CD19 binding to CAR-expressing cells and reported apparent KD values of 0.22-1.8 nM depending on incubation time and ligand depletion conditions. Scientific Reports, 13:23024. PMC10754921.

## 6. 2D Kinetics Platforms

### Why 2D Kinetics Matter
All the techniques described above (SPR, BLI, MST, ITC) measure **3D binding kinetics** -- at least one binding partner is in free solution, and binding parameters have units reflecting 3D concentration (M, M^-1, etc.). However, in the biological context of a CAR T cell interacting with a tumor cell, **both the CAR and the target antigen are anchored in opposing cell membranes**, making the interaction inherently **two-dimensional**.

Key differences between 2D and 3D kinetics:
- **2D affinity units:** micromoles per square micrometer (not M^-1)
- **2D kon units:** micrometers^2 per second (not M^-1 s^-1)
- **2D koff units:** s^-1 (same as 3D)
- **2D off-rates can be >8,000-fold faster** than 3D off-rates for the same receptor-ligand pair (Huang et al., 2010, Nature)
- **2D parameters correlate better with T cell function** than 3D parameters (Huang et al., 2010)
- **2D kinetics are influenced by** external force, membrane fluctuation, receptor/ligand flexibility, molecular confinement, and cytoskeletal engagement -- none of which are captured by 3D measurements

**Source:** Huang et al. (2010) Nature 464:932-936; PMC2925443. Chesla et al. (1998) Biophysical Journal. Zhu et al. (2013) Frontiers in Immunology.

### 6.1 Micropipette Adhesion Frequency Assay

**Principle:** Two cells are held by opposing micropipettes. One cell expresses the receptor (e.g., TCR or CAR); the other cell or a red blood cell (RBC, used as a flexible adhesion sensor) presents the ligand. The cells are brought into contact for a defined duration, then separated. Whether adhesion occurred (a binary yes/no event) is scored. By repeating this contact-retraction cycle hundreds of times at different contact durations, an adhesion frequency (Pa) vs. contact time curve is generated.

**What it measures:**
- **2D effective affinity** (AcKa, where Ac is the contact area)
- **2D on-rate** (Ac kon): from the initial slope of the adhesion frequency vs. contact time curve
- **2D off-rate** (koff): inversely proportional to the half-time to reach steady-state adhesion

**Key features:**
- Uses an RBC as a highly sensitive adhesion sensor (due to its deformability)
- Measures force-free or near-force-free binding
- Can measure extremely fast kinetics (sub-second off-rates) with appropriate temporal resolution (~0.2 s limit)
- Requires micromanipulation expertise and specialized equipment

**Landmark study:** Huang et al. (2010) Nature 464:932-936. Using micropipette adhesion frequency and BFP thermal fluctuation assays on OT-1 CD8+ T cells with a panel of pMHC ligands:
- 2D affinities showed **4-log dynamic range** (vs. ~1.5-log for 3D SPR)
- 2D off-rates were **up to 8,300-fold faster** than 3D off-rates
- 2D kinetic parameters **correlated with T cell responses** (cytotoxicity, proliferation), while 3D parameters did not

**Source:** Huang et al. (2010) Nature 464:932-936, PMID: 20357766, PMC2925443.

### 6.2 Biomembrane Force Probe (BFP)

**Principle:** Similar to micropipette adhesion, but a BFP uses an RBC held by a micropipette as a calibrated force transducer. A bead coated with ligand is attached to the apex of the RBC. The RBC's known spring constant allows measurement of the force applied to individual bonds during retraction. Additionally, the BFP thermal fluctuation method monitors reduction in RBC fluctuations when a bond forms and resumption when the bond breaks, enabling detection of individual bond formation/dissociation events in real time.

**What it measures:**
- **Bond lifetime under force** -- critical for distinguishing catch bonds (strengthen under force) from slip bonds (weaken under force)
- **2D on-rate and off-rate** (via thermal fluctuation mode)
- **Single-bond rupture forces**
- **Force-dependent kinetics** -- how koff changes with applied force

**Key applications:**
- Liu et al. (2014) European Journal of Immunology 44:205-213: Used micropipette adhesion and BFP to show that 2D TCR-pMHC-CD8 kinetics determine T cell responses for self-antigen-specific TCR system
- Used to demonstrate catch-bond behavior of TCR-pMHC (bond lifetime increases under ~10 pN force)

**Source:** Zarnitsyna & Zhu (2012) Methods Mol Biol 748:151-163 (PMC3308619); Chen et al. (2008) Journal of Cell Biology; Ju et al. (2015) PMC4544851.

### 6.3 Acoustic Force Spectroscopy

**Principle:** Uses acoustic waves (MHz frequency) to apply calibrated forces to beads or cells in a microfluidic chamber. Standing acoustic waves create pressure nodes that trap and manipulate particles. By ramping acoustic force, one can measure the force required to break molecular bonds, yielding unbinding kinetics under force.

**Specialized variant for CAR T cells -- LUMICKS z-Movi Cell Avidity Analyzer:**
- A piezoelectric element generates acoustic force on T cells adhered to a monolayer of target cells
- Ramps force from 0 to ~1 nN; fluorescence microscopy tracks individual T cells detaching as force increases
- Generates a **cell avidity distribution** -- the fraction of cells remaining bound at each force level
- Can measure hundreds of cells in parallel (high throughput for a single-cell method)

**What z-Movi measures:**
- **Cell avidity** (not molecular affinity) -- integrates contributions of receptor density, clustering, cytoskeletal engagement
- **Avidity distributions** across cell populations
- **Correlates with in vivo efficacy** of CAR T cells (LUMICKS application data)

**Source:** LUMICKS z-Movi technology page; Nature (d42473-020-00095-y); LUMICKS Cancer Cell paper on AML CAR T avidity enhancement.

**IMPORTANT NOTE:** z-Movi is a specialized instrument (~$200,000-$400,000 estimated) not yet widely available in academic labs, especially in India. It measures cell avidity, not molecular kinetics per se.

### 6.4 How 2D Kinetics Relate to 3D SPR/BLI Measurements

| Aspect | 3D (SPR/BLI) | 2D (Micropipette/BFP) |
|--------|-------------|----------------------|
| **KD units** | M (moles/L) | um^4 (effective 2D affinity x contact area) |
| **kon units** | M^-1 s^-1 | um^2 s^-1 (or um^4 s^-1 with contact area) |
| **koff units** | s^-1 | s^-1 |
| **Dynamic range** | ~1-2 log (for TCR-pMHC panel) | ~4 log |
| **Correlation with T cell function** | Poor (for TCR-pMHC) | Good |
| **Force dependence** | Not measured | Can measure force-dependent kinetics |
| **Membrane effects** | Not captured | Captured (confinement, diffusion, fluctuation) |

**Critical insight:** There is **no simple conversion formula** between 2D and 3D kinetic parameters. 2D kinetics are not simply 3D kinetics divided by a confinement factor. Molecular diffusion, linker flexibility, membrane fluctuations, cytoskeletal coupling, and receptor clustering all contribute to 2D kinetics in ways not predictable from 3D measurements alone.

**Source:** Zhu et al. (2013) Frontiers in Immunology review; Huang et al. (2010) Nature; Chesla et al. (1998) Biophysical Journal.

### 6.5 2D Kinetics for CARs -- Current State of Literature

**IMPORTANT: No published studies measuring 2D kinetics (micropipette adhesion or BFP) for CAR-CD19 or any scFv-based CAR interaction were found.** The 2D kinetics literature is almost entirely focused on TCR-pMHC interactions.

The closest related work:
- **Dong et al. (2024)** "Mechanical force determines chimeric antigen receptor microclustering and signaling." Molecular Therapy 32:1-16. PMID: 38327049. This study used DNA-based tension gauge tethers (12-51 pN) to study how mechanical force regulates CAR microclustering, but **did not use micropipette or BFP methods** and **did not measure classical 2D kinetic parameters (2D kon, koff, affinity).**
- **LUMICKS z-Movi** has published CAR T cell avidity data (acoustic force-based) but this measures cell avidity distributions, not 2D molecular kinetics.

**This represents a significant gap in the literature and a potential area of novelty for this project** -- measuring 2D kinetics of CAR-CD19 interaction using micropipette or BFP methods has not been reported.

## 8. Published FMC63-CD19 Binding Kinetics

### Comprehensive Literature Summary

The FMC63 scFv is the most widely used binder in approved CD19-targeting CAR T products (Kymriah, Yescarta, Tecartus, Breyanzi). Despite this, its binding affinity was not unambiguously determined until 2023. Published values differ by >100-fold due to experimental artifacts.

#### Key Study 1: Seigner et al. (2023) Scientific Reports

**Citation:** Seigner J, Zajc CU, Dotsch S, Eigner C, Laurent E, Busch DH, Lehner M, Traxlmayr MW. "Solving the mystery of the FMC63-CD19 affinity." Scientific Reports 13:23024 (2023). DOI: 10.1038/s41598-023-48528-0. PMID: 38155191. PMC10754921.

**Instrument:** Biacore T200 (GE Healthcare/Cytiva)
**Chip:** Biotin CAPture S Series sensor chip (Cytiva)
**CD19 construct:** Tag-free, stability-engineered soluble CD19 (SF-CD19) with three point mutations (M75V, R76S, F85S) to enable monomeric purification; >99% monomer by HPLC-SEC-MALS.

**Definitive SPR results (optimized conditions):**

| Condition | KD (nM) | kon (M^-1 s^-1) | koff (s^-1) |
|-----------|---------|-----------------|-------------|
| FMC63-scFv immobilized, tag-free SF-CD19 analyte | **5.1** | **1.0 x 10^5** | **5.3 x 10^-4** |
| SF-CD19 immobilized, FMC63-scFv analyte | **0.91** | Not reported separately | Not reported separately |
| FLEXamer variant | **2.7** | Not reported separately | Not reported separately |

**Range of values obtained under different conditions in this study:**

| Method | CD19 Form | KD (nM) | Key Artifact |
|--------|-----------|---------|--------------|
| Flow cytometry, 0.5 h incubation | NALM6 cells (WT CD19) | 0.22 | Ligand depletion + insufficient equilibration |
| Flow cytometry, 4 h incubation | NALM6 cells (WT CD19) | 1.8 | Longer equilibration reduces artifact |
| Flow cytometry, 4 h | Soluble His8-SUMO-SF-CD19 on CAR T cells | 0.79 | Minor oligomerization of fusion construct |
| SPR, ligand immobilized | Tag-free SF-CD19 analyte | **5.1** | Minimal artifacts (optimized) |
| SPR, inverted orientation | FMC63-scFv analyte | 0.91 | scFv dimerization (~20%) causes avidity |

**Overall conclusion:** The "true" monovalent affinity of FMC63 for CD19 is **2-6 nM** when artifacts are minimized.

**Key artifacts identified (causing published values to range 0.22-47 nM):**
1. **Insufficient incubation time** (0.5-1 h instead of 4+ h) -- underestimates KD
2. **Ligand depletion** at low nM concentrations -- overestimates affinity
3. **Avidity effects** from scFv dimerization or multivalent display -- overestimates affinity
4. **CD19 oligomerization** when using tagged/fusion constructs -- overestimates affinity

#### Key Study 2: He et al. (2023) Science Immunology

**Citation:** He C et al. "CD19 CAR antigen engagement mechanisms and affinity tuning." Science Immunology 8:eadf1426 (2023). DOI: 10.1126/sciimmunol.adf1426. PMID: 36867678. PMC10228544.

**Method:** SPR (instrument not specified in search results; likely Biacore)
**CD19 construct:** Soluble CD19 ectodomain

**FMC63-CD19 kinetics:**
| Binder | KD (nM) |
|--------|---------|
| **FMC63 wild-type** | **4.5** |
| SJ25C1 (alternative anti-CD19 scFv) | 26.9 |

**Affinity-tuned FMC63 mutants (lower affinity):**
| Variant | KD (nM) | Fold reduction |
|---------|---------|----------------|
| FMC63-Y70A | 275.3 | ~61x |
| FMC63-Y261A | 682.5 | ~152x |
| FMC63-Y260A | No binding (>5000) | >1000x |

**Affinity-tuned SJ25C1 mutants:**
| Variant | KD (nM) | Notes |
|---------|---------|-------|
| SJ25C1-I120V | 285.5 | Reduced affinity |
| SJ25C1-W52F | 220.2 | Reduced affinity |
| SJ25C1-I120A | No binding | Lost binding |
| SJ25C1-S121A | Increased vs. WT | Higher affinity than WT |

This study also solved cryo-EM structures of FMC63-CD19 (PDB: 7URV) and SJ25C1-CD19 (PDB: 7URX), enabling structure-guided affinity engineering.

#### Summary of All Published FMC63-CD19 KD Values

| Study | Year | Method | KD (nM) | Notes |
|-------|------|--------|---------|-------|
| Seigner et al. | 2023 | SPR (Biacore T200) | **5.1** (range: 2-6) | Optimized, tag-free monomeric CD19; most rigorous |
| He et al. | 2023 | SPR | **4.5** | Cryo-EM guided study |
| Various earlier studies | Pre-2023 | Various (SPR, flow cytometry) | **0.3 to 47** | Artifacts: ligand depletion, avidity, insufficient equilibration |
| Seigner et al. (flow cytometry) | 2023 | Flow cytometry | 0.22-1.8 | Demonstrating artifacts |

**Consensus:** FMC63 wild-type KD for CD19 is approximately **2-6 nM** by SPR under optimized conditions. The value of 5.1 nM (Seigner et al., 2023) and 4.5 nM (He et al., 2023) are the most reliable published values.

**VERIFICATION STATUS for user query:** "Guedan et al. 2023 reported FMC63 KD of 2-6 nM by SPR" -- The 2-6 nM range is correct, but the first author is **Seigner, not Guedan**. The full citation is Seigner et al. (2023) Scientific Reports 13:23024. Traxlmayr is the corresponding/senior author. The paper is from the University of Natural Resources and Life Sciences, Vienna, Austria.

## 9. Recommendation for This Project

### Primary Platform: SPR (Biacore T200)

**Rationale:**
1. **Provides both kon and koff** -- essential for this project since FMC63 variants generated by site-saturation mutagenesis may differ in kinetic profile (fast-on/fast-off vs. slow-on/slow-off) even if KD values are similar. **Kinetic profiles, not just equilibrium affinity, determine CAR T cell function.**
2. **Benchmark data exists** -- FMC63-CD19 KD of 5.1 nM was measured on Biacore T200, allowing direct comparison with published values
3. **Gold standard** for binding kinetics in the CAR T field
4. **Appropriate sensitivity** for the expected KD range (low nM for WT FMC63; high nM to uM for reduced-affinity variants)

### Secondary/Complementary Platform: BLI (Octet)

**Rationale:**
1. Higher throughput for initial screening of large numbers of variants from site-saturation mutagenesis library
2. Provides same kinetic parameters (kon, koff, KD)
3. Lower maintenance; works with less pure samples
4. Use for initial ranking, then confirm top hits by SPR

### Validation: Flow Cytometry

**Rationale:**
1. Already available at CSIR-IGIB
2. Confirms that SPR/BLI results translate to binding on cell surface
3. The Seigner et al. (2023) study explicitly showed how flow cytometry apparent KD can differ from SPR KD, providing a framework for interpreting discrepancies
4. Doublet formation assay (Galvez-Cancino et al., 2024) can assess functional avidity of CAR variants

### Suggested Experimental Workflow

```
Site-saturation mutagenesis library
           |
           v
   Express/purify scFv variants
           |
           v
   [1] BLI (Octet) -- initial screening
       Rank variants by KD
       Identify top hits (e.g., 10-20 variants spanning KD range)
           |
           v
   [2] SPR (Biacore T200) -- detailed kinetics
       Full kinetic characterization (kon, koff, KD)
       Compare to WT FMC63 (KD ~5 nM benchmark)
           |
           v
   [3] Flow cytometry -- cell surface binding
       Confirm binding to CD19+ cells (NALM6, Raji)
       Measure apparent KD on cells
           |
           v
   [4] Functional assays with CAR T cells
       Correlate affinity/kinetics with T cell activation, cytotoxicity, exhaustion
```

### If Only One Platform is Available: SPR

If budget or access constraints limit to a single platform, SPR (Biacore T200) is the clear choice. It is the only platform that provides both the kinetic data needed (kon, koff) and the benchmarked comparability to published FMC63-CD19 values.
